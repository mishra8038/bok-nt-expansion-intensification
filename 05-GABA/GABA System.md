# The GABA System

GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is the brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter. It is the brake that filters distracting input, prevents global excitotoxicity, and keeps the excitatory systems (glutamate, dopamine) from over-firing. It operates through two receptor families: **$GABA_A$** (fast, ionotropic, chloride channel) and **$GABA_B$** (slower, metabotropic, potassium channel). This folder holds the two protocols for working with it.

The GABA system is the counterweight to the excitatory systems: too much GABA produces sedation and cognitive dulling, too little produces anxiety and excitotoxicity. The schedules in this folder are built around careful dosing, clear off-cycles, and the 4-to-6-week upregulation lag for expansion.
