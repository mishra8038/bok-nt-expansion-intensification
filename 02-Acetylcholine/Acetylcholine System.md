# The Acetylcholine System

Acetylcholine (ACh) is the neurotransmitter of attention, encoding, and signal-to-noise. It sharpens the signal relative to the noise, drives the depth of focus, and is essential for spatial memory and the consolidation of new information. It operates through two receptor families: **nicotinic** (fast, ionotropic, at the synapse) and **muscarinic** (slower, metabotropic, driving cortical attention cascades). This folder holds the two protocols for working with it.

The cholinergic system is also the one most sensitive to over-stimulation: too much ACh at once produces a cholinergic crisis (nausea, bradycardia, muscle tension), so the schedules in this folder are built around careful dosing and clear off-cycles.
