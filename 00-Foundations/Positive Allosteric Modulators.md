# Positive Allosteric Modulators

Positive allosteric modulators (PAMs) are the gold standard for real-world nootropic architectures, and the reason is a single property: **they do not fire the receptor artificially.** A direct agonist slams the receptor open regardless of whether the natural transmitter is present, which is exactly what triggers homeostatic down-regulation. A PAM instead binds to a non-orthosteric site and *amplifies* the effect of the natural transmitter when it arrives. The result is that the network keeps its natural pulsatile signaling rhythm while its structural response is significantly amplified. That is why PAMs are the preferred tool for both reinforcing and expanding a network without crashing it.

The master table below maps each system to its target receptor and its primary PAM. The "real-world" column notes the mechanism, and the "OTC / herbal / supplement" column is the substitution this book uses wherever the original framework named a prescription-only or research compound.

| System | Target receptor | Primary PAM (framework) | OTC / herbal / supplement substitute | Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Glutamate | AMPA | Aniracetam / Piracetam | **Piracetam** (OTC in many regions) or **Lion's Mane** | Slows receptor deactivation and desensitization, easing $Mg^{2+}$ displacement from NMDA |
| Glutamate | NMDA | Neboglamine / Magnesium L-Threonate | **Magnesium L-Threonate** (OTC supplement) | Enhances glycine-site binding affinity, priming the NMDA channel for LTP influx |
| Acetylcholine | Nicotinic ($\alpha7$, $\alpha4\beta2$) | Galantamine / Cotinine | **Galantamine** (OTC supplement) / **Cotinine** (from tobacco, avoid) | Binds non-orthosteric sites to raise channel-opening probability when natural ACh binds |
| Acetylcholine | Muscarinic (M1) | Benzylquinolone / Sabcomeline | **Huperzine A** (OTC herbal) | Enhances M1 signaling cascades, driving cortical attention pathways |
| Dopamine | D1 / D5 | DETQ / SKF-83959 | **L-Tyrosine** (amino acid) + **Mucuna pruriens** (herbal) | Potentiates intracellular cAMP accumulation, multiplying signaling efficiency of low dopamine |
| Dopamine | D2 / D3 | *Vitex agnus-castus* methanol extract | **Chasteberry / Vitex** (OTC herbal) | Modulates D2 binding dynamics, smoothing motor and focus pathways |
| Serotonin | 5-HT1A | Cannabidiol (CBD) | **CBD** (OTC where legal) / **Kanna** (herbal) | Enhances 5-HT1A activation under low baseline, driving quick anxiolysis |
| Serotonin | 5-HT2C | Tramadol metabolites (selective) | **5-HTP** (amino acid) — use cautiously | Shifts affinity properties of natural serotonin profiles |
| GABA | $GABA_A$ ($\alpha1,2,3,5$) | Honokiol / Magnolol (Magnolia bark) | **Magnolia bark extract** (OTC herbal) | Binds distinct subunits to lengthen chloride-channel openings from endogenous GABA |
| GABA | $GABA_A$ (benzodiazepine site) | Baicalein / Apigenin | **Baicalein** (Scutellaria) / **Apigenin** (chamomile) — OTC herbals | Raises receptor binding affinity for GABA, smooth inhibition without heavy sedation |

Two practical notes. First, the PAM is almost always the *second* thing you take in a reinforce stack — it comes after the precursor has loaded the intracellular pool, per the half-life rule. Second, several of the framework's "PAMs" are actually research compounds (SKF-83959, DETQ, Sabcomeline) that are not available OTC; the substitutes in the table are the closest legal, purchasable analogs, and they are weaker, so the schedules in each chapter compensate with longer cycles rather than higher doses.
