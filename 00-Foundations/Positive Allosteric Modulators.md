# Positive Allosteric Modulators

Positive allosteric modulators (PAMs) are the gold standard for real-world nootropic architectures, and the reason is a single property: **they do not fire the receptor artificially.** A direct agonist slams the receptor open regardless of whether the natural transmitter is present, which is exactly what triggers homeostatic down-regulation. A PAM instead binds to a non-orthosteric site and *amplifies* the effect of the natural transmitter when it arrives. The result is that the network keeps its natural pulsatile signaling rhythm while its structural response is significantly amplified. That is why PAMs are the preferred tool for both reinforcing and expanding a network without crashing it.

## Practical Notes

1. **The PAM is almost always the *second* thing you take in a reinforce stack** — it comes after the precursor has loaded the intracellular pool, per the half-life rule.
2. **Several of the framework's "PAMs" are actually research compounds** (SKF-83959, DETQ, Sabcomeline, Neboglamine) that are not available OTC; the substitutes in the tables are the closest legal, purchasable analogs, and they are weaker, so the schedules in each chapter compensate with longer cycles rather than higher doses.
3. **Not all agents in the tables are true PAMs.** Some are precursors (L-Tyrosine, 5-HTP, L-Tryptophan, Arachidonic Acid), some are agonists (L-Glycine, D-serine), and some are indirect modulators (adaptogens, choline sources). The tables include all of them because they all contribute to the same goal: enhancing the natural signaling of the target receptor without artificially firing it.
4. **The OTC column is a guide, not a guarantee.** Availability varies by country and regulatory environment. Always check local regulations before purchasing.
