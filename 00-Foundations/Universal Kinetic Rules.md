# Universal Kinetic Rules

Three rules govern any stack in this book. They are not optional tuning details; they are the difference between a stack that builds structure and one that crashes the network. Every schedule in every chapter is built to obey them.

**1. The Half-Life Rule.** Precursor loading must peak **60 to 90 minutes before** a synaptic signal enhancer or PAM is introduced. The logic: a breakdown inhibitor (an AChE inhibitor, an MAO-B inhibitor, a reuptake blocker) only works if there is already transmitter in the pool to preserve. If you introduce the inhibitor while the intracellular pool is depleted, the enzyme sits empty and you get acute synaptic depletion — the "crash." So in every reinforce schedule, the precursor (L-Tyrosine, 5-HTP, Alpha-GPC, L-Theanine) is taken first, and the enhancer/PAM is timed to land 60–90 minutes later, at the peak of the precursor's effect.

**2. The 4-to-6-Week Upregulation Lag.** Real network expansion is an architectural phenomenon that requires continuous, low-intensity genetic transcription. A short, massive burst of direct agonists yields only temporary hyper-focus followed by rapid homeostatic down-regulation. True growth takes over a month of cyclical, systematic prompts. This is why every **Expansion** schedule in this book runs for 4–6 weeks minimum, with intermittent antagonist phases and growth-stimulator phases, rather than a single high-dose push.
**3. The Stimulant Burnout Axiom.** Combining heavy NMDA receptor enhancers alongside powerful stimulants bypasses the brain's baseline safety limits. The resulting unregulated influx of glutamate and monoamines bypasses cellular defense thresholds, precipitating excitotoxicity, immediate brain fog, and severe synaptic damage instead of long-term memory reinforcement. In practice: never stack a strong NMDA/AMPA enhancer with a strong stimulant (caffeine at high dose, amphetamines, modafinil) in the same window. If you use a stimulant, keep the glutamate-side enhancers mild and well-separated in time.

## Interaction Warnings

The three rules above govern *timing within a single stack*. The warnings below govern *interactions between systems* — they are the cross-system safety net that prevents one protocol from undermining another.

### Histamine Gating

Histamine (see [[Modulatory Systems]]) gates the release of acetylcholine and dopamine in the cortex and striatum. This means:

- **Avoid antihistamines** (diphenhydramine, cetirizine, loratadine) during [[Acetylcholine Intensification]] or [[Dopamine Intensification]] protocols. Antihistamines blunt histamine-mediated gating, reducing the efficacy of the ACh and dopamine stacks.
- **L-Histidine** (500–1000 mg) can be added to ACh or dopamine stacks to support histamine tone, but only if you are not already taking an antihistamine.

### Norepinephrine Inverted-U

Norepinephrine (see [[Norepinephrine System]]) follows an inverted-U relationship with cognitive performance: too little = inattention; too much = anxiety and cognitive rigidity. This means:

- **Do not stack** [[Norepinephrine Intensification]] with [[Dopamine Intensification]] in the same window. Both systems share the L-Tyrosine precursor, and stacking them can push NE past the peak of the inverted-U, producing anxiety and rigidity instead of focus.
- **Monitor for over-stimulation** when running NE protocols: anxiety, insomnia, elevated heart rate, or blood pressure. If these occur, reduce the dose or take an off-cycle sooner.
- **Avoid combining** high-dose L-Phenylalanine or L-Tyrosine with MAO inhibitors (e.g. Selegiline) without careful monitoring, as this can precipitate a hypertensive crisis.

### Endocannabinoid Tone-Setting

The endocannabinoid system (see [[Endocannabinoid System]]) is the brain's retrograde tone-setter: it modulates the release of every other NT. This means:

- **CBD and CBG** (used in [[Endocannabinoid Intensification]]) can blunt the efficacy of other protocols if taken in the same window. Space eCB protocols at least 4 hours from other Intensification or Expansion protocols.
- **Arachidonic Acid** (used in eCB protocols) can increase inflammation in sensitive individuals. If you are running a [[Glutamate Intensification]] or [[Glutamate Expansion]] protocol, monitor for signs of neuroinflammation (brain fog, irritability) and reduce AA if necessary.

### Endogenous Opioid Reward Coupling

The endogenous opioid system (see [[Endogenous Opioid System]]) is tightly coupled to the dopamine reward circuit. This means:

- **Do not stack** [[Endogenous Opioid Intensification]] with [[Dopamine Intensification]] in the same window. The μ-opioid receptors in the VTA disinhibit dopamine neurons; stacking both protocols can produce an exaggerated reward response followed by a crash.
- **Aerobic exercise** (the behavioral trigger in the opioid protocol) is the most potent natural trigger for endorphin release, but it also elevates NE and dopamine. If you are running an NE or dopamine protocol on the same day, do the exercise at least 4 hours before the supplement stack.

### Glycine as the NMDA Gate

Glycine (see [[Glycine System]]) is the obligate co-agonist of the NMDA receptor. This means:

- **The [[Glutamate Intensification]] and [[Glutamate Expansion]] protocols implicitly depend on adequate glycine.** If glycine levels are low, the NMDA channel will not open, and the LTP machinery stalls regardless of how much glutamate or AMPA PAM you load.
- **L-Glycine** (1–3 g) can be added to any glutamate protocol to ensure the NMDA co-agonist site is saturated.
- **Taurine** (500–1000 mg) is a PAM of the $GlyR$ and can be added to GABA protocols to enhance inhibitory tone.

### Cross-System Frequency Rule

- **One system at a time.** Do not run two Intensification or two Expansion protocols in the same week. The homeostatic dial is shared across systems, and stacking two protocols at once produces unpredictable down-regulation.
- **Intensify first, then expand.** Run a 4–6 week Intensification cycle to strengthen existing synapses, then a 4–6 week Expansion cycle to build new structure. Do not run them concurrently.
- **Off-cycles are mandatory.** After every 4–6 week cycle, take a 1–2 week off-cycle with no agents from that system. This is how the network consolidates and avoids tolerance.
