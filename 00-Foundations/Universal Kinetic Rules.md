# Universal Kinetic Rules

Three rules govern any stack in this book. They are not optional tuning details; they are the difference between a stack that builds structure and one that crashes the network. Every schedule in every chapter is built to obey them.

**1. The Half-Life Rule.** Precursor loading must peak **60 to 90 minutes before** a synaptic signal enhancer or PAM is introduced. The logic: a breakdown inhibitor (an AChE inhibitor, an MAO-B inhibitor, a reuptake blocker) only works if there is already transmitter in the pool to preserve. If you introduce the inhibitor while the intracellular pool is depleted, the enzyme sits empty and you get acute synaptic depletion — the "crash." So in every reinforce schedule, the precursor (L-Tyrosine, 5-HTP, Alpha-GPC, L-Theanine) is taken first, and the enhancer/PAM is timed to land 60–90 minutes later, at the peak of the precursor's effect.

**2. The 4-to-6-Week Upregulation Lag.** Real network expansion is an architectural phenomenon that requires continuous, low-intensity genetic transcription. A short, massive burst of direct agonists yields only temporary hyper-focus followed by rapid homeostatic down-regulation. True growth takes over a month of cyclical, systematic prompts. This is why every **Expansion** schedule in this book runs for 4–6 weeks minimum, with intermittent antagonist phases and growth-stimulator phases, rather than a single high-dose push.

**3. The Stimulant Burnout Axiom.** Combining heavy NMDA receptor enhancers alongside powerful stimulants bypasses the brain's baseline safety limits. The resulting unregulated influx of glutamate and monoamines bypasses cellular defense thresholds, precipitating excitotoxicity, immediate brain fog, and severe synaptic damage instead of long-term memory reinforcement. In practice: never stack a strong NMDA/AMPA enhancer with a strong stimulant (caffeine at high dose, amphetamines, modafinil) in the same window. If you use a stimulant, keep the glutamate-side enhancers mild and well-separated in time.
