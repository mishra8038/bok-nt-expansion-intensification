# Modulatory Systems

Beyond the nine full systems in this book (glutamate, acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin, GABA, glycine, norepinephrine, endocannabinoid, endogenous opioid), one additional transmitter — **histamine** — is directly loadable as an OTC supplement and modulates the major systems in ways that affect protocol efficacy. It does not warrant a full Intensification/Expansion protocol of its own, but it is important to understand when designing stacks.

> [!note] Norepinephrine
> Norepinephrine was originally covered here as a modulatory system. It has since been promoted to a full system with its own Intensification and Expansion protocols. See [[Norepinephrine System]] for the complete treatment.

## Histamine

Histamine is the brain's primary wakefulness and attention transmitter. It is released by the tuberomammillary nucleus and projects widely to the cortex, hippocampus, and basal forebrain.

### Roles

- **Wakefulness and attention.** Histamine is the "arousal" transmitter; antihistamines (e.g. diphenhydramine) cause drowsiness by blocking H1 receptors.
- **Gating of ACh and dopamine.** Histamine gates the release of acetylcholine and dopamine in the cortex and striatum. Low histamine = blunted ACh and dopamine signaling.
- **Neuroplasticity.** Histamine H1 and H4 receptors modulate BDNF expression and synaptic plasticity.

### OTC / Supplement Agents

| Agent | Class | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| L-Histidine | Amino acid | Precursor for histamine synthesis |
| Quercetin | Flavonoid | Mast-cell stabilizer; reduces histamine breakdown |
| Vitamin C | Vitamin | Cofactor for histamine synthesis |

### Relationship to Other Systems

- **Acetylcholine.** Histamine gates ACh release in the cortex. The [[Acetylcholine Intensification]] protocol is blunted by antihistamines or low histamine.
- **Dopamine.** Histamine gates dopamine release in the striatum. The [[Dopamine Intensification]] protocol is blunted by antihistamines or low histamine.
- **Glutamate.** Histamine modulates glutamatergic transmission in the cortex.

### Safety

- L-Histidine is generally safe at 500–1000 mg.
- Excess histamine can cause headaches, flushing, or GI discomfort.
- **Avoid antihistamines** (e.g. diphenhydramine, cetirizine) during ACh or Dopamine protocols, as they blunt histamine-mediated gating.
