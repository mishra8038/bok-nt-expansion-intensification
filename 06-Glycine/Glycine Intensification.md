# Glycine Intensification

Glycine intensification reinforces existing glycine-mediated signaling: it strengthens the NMDA co-agonist pathway (unlocking LTP) and the $GlyR$ inhibitory pathway (calming, sleep, spinal inhibition). The goal is to ensure that the NMDA receptor is never "silent" due to glycine limitation, and that inhibitory tone is robust without over-sedation.

## Protocol

The sequence follows the universal kinetic rules in [[Universal Kinetic Rules]]: load the precursor first, then introduce the signal enhancer 60–90 minutes later.

1. **Load the precursor.** L-Glycine 1–3 g (fasted or with a small meal). This raises the intracellular glycine pool and ensures the NMDA co-agonist site is saturated.
2. **Introduce the signal enhancer (60–90 min later).** Taurine 500–1000 mg (substitutes for glycine at the NMDA co-agonist site, extending the window of NMDA availability) + Magnesium L-Threonate 1.5 g (NMDA channel modulator; works synergistically with glycine).
3. **Optional: GABA co-loading.** If the goal is to potentiate inhibitory tone (e.g. for sleep or anxiety), add L-Theanine 100–200 mg + Magnesium Glycinate 200–400 mg with the precursor. This bridges into the [[GABA Intensification]] protocol.

## Cycle

- **Duration:** 4–6 weeks.
- **Off-cycle:** 1–2 weeks with no glycine or taurine.
- **Do not combine** with high-dose NMDA agonists (e.g. high-dose Piracetam) in the same window — see [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] for the stimulant burnout axiom.

## Safety

- Glycine is one of the safest supplements. Typical doses are 1–3 g.
- High doses (>5 g) can cause mild drowsiness or GI discomfort.
- Because glycine is an NMDA co-agonist, it should not be combined with high-dose NMDA agonists without careful timing.
