# Glycine System

Glycine is the smallest amino acid and one of the brain's two major inhibitory transmitters (alongside GABA). It is also the **obligate co-agonist of the NMDA receptor**: the NMDA channel will not open unless both glutamate (at the agonist site) and glycine (at the co-agonist site) are bound, and the magnesium block is relieved. Without sufficient glycine, the entire LTP machinery described in [[Glutamate System]] stalls — the NMDA receptor is silent even when glutamate is abundant.

## Roles

- **NMDA co-agonist.** Glycine binds the glycine site on the NR1 subunit of the NMDA receptor. This is a prerequisite for calcium influx and LTP. Low glycine = silent NMDA = no plasticity.
- **Inhibitory transmitter.** Glycine receptors ($GlyR$) are chloride channels, functionally analogous to $GABA_A$ receptors. They provide fast inhibition, especially in the spinal cord, brainstem, and cerebellum.
- **GABAergic modulation.** Glycine and GABA often co-localize and co-release; glycine can potentiate GABAergic inhibition in certain circuits.
- **Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant.** Glycine is a precursor for glutathione and porphyrins, and it has mild anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Thermoregulation.** Oral glycine lowers core body temperature and improves sleep quality.

## Receptors

| Receptor | Type | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| NMDA receptor (glycine site) | Ionotropic (co-agonist) | Prerequisite for NMDA channel opening and LTP |
| $GlyR$ (Glycine receptor) | Ionotropic (Cl⁻ channel) | Fast inhibition, especially in spinal cord and brainstem |
| Taurine/glycine site on NMDA | Ionotropic (co-agonist) | Taurine can substitute for glycine at this site |

## OTC / Supplement Agents

| Agent | Class | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| L-Glycine | Amino acid | Direct glycine precursor; NMDA co-agonist + inhibitory transmitter |
| Taurine | Amino sulfonic acid | Can substitute for glycine at the NMDA co-agonist site |
| Magnesium | Mineral | NMDA channel blocker (relieved by depolarization); works synergistically with glycine |

## Relationship to Other Systems

- **Glutamate.** Glycine is the co-agonist that unlocks the NMDA receptor. The [[Glutamate Intensification]] and [[Glutamate Expansion]] protocols implicitly depend on adequate glycine.
- **GABA.** Glycine and GABA are both inhibitory; they often co-release and can potentiate each other. The [[GABA Intensification]] protocol benefits from glycine co-loading.
- **Dopamine.** Glycine modulates dopaminergic signaling in the striatum; low glycine can blunt dopamine's effects.

## Safety

- Glycine is one of the safest supplements available. Typical doses are 1–3 g.
- High doses (>5 g) can cause mild drowsiness or GI discomfort.
- Because glycine is an NMDA co-agonist, it should **not** be combined with high-dose NMDA agonists (e.g. high-dose AMPA PAMs) without careful timing — see [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] for the stimulant burnout axiom.
