# Glycine Expansion

Glycine expansion builds new glycine-mediated structure: it increases $GlyR$ receptor density (more inhibitory synapses) and strengthens the NMDA co-agonist pathway (more robust LTP capacity). The goal is to create a brain that has both robust inhibitory tone and a highly responsive NMDA/LTP system.

## Protocol

The sequence follows the universal kinetic rules in [[Universal Kinetic Rules]]: intermittent antagonism to trigger upregulation, then growth stimulation, then dense influx.

1. **Intermittent antagonism (days 1–5).** Reduce glycine loading for 2–3 days (e.g. skip L-Glycine) to trigger homeostatic upregulation of $GlyR$ receptors. This is the "starve to grow" phase.
2. **Growth stimulation (daily).** Lion's Mane 1000 mg + Uridine Monophosphate (UMP) 250–500 mg + DHA 500–1000 mg. These neurotrophic factors drive synaptogenesis at $GlyR$ sites and strengthen the NMDA co-agonist pathway.
3. **Dense influx (days 6+).** Resume L-Glycine 1–3 g + Taurine 500–1000 mg + Magnesium L-Threonate 1.5 g. This floods the newly upregulated receptors with glycine, locking in the structural gains.

## Cycle

- **Duration:** 4–6 weeks.
- **Off-cycle:** 1–2 weeks with no glycine or taurine.
- **Do not combine** with high-dose NMDA agonists in the same window — see [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] for the stimulant burnout axiom.

## Safety

- Glycine is one of the safest supplements. Typical doses are 1–3 g.
- The intermittent antagonism phase (days 1–5) should not exceed 3 days without glycine, to avoid NMDA "silence" and impaired plasticity.
- Because glycine is an NMDA co-agonist, it should not be combined with high-dose NMDA agonists without careful timing.
