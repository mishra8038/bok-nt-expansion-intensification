# The Glutamate System

Glutamate is the brain's primary excitatory neurotransmitter, and it is the engine of learning, memory, and long-term potentiation (LTP). It operates via a delicate equilibrium between immediate performance and long-term neuroprotection: too little, and memory traces never consolidate; too much, and you cross into excitotoxicity. This folder holds the two protocols for working with it.

**The cellular machinery of glutamate LTP.** At rest, the NMDA receptor is physically blocked by a tightly bound magnesium ($Mg^{2+}$) ion — a gatekeeper that prevents arbitrary electrical noise from flooding the post-synaptic neuron. When glutamate is released into the cleft, it first hits the AMPA receptors, which open instantly and let sodium ($Na^+$) in. As $Na^+$ builds inside the post-synaptic cell, the shifting positive interior voltage physically repels and expels the $Mg^{2+}$ plug out of the NMDA channel. The channel opens, calcium ($Ca^{2+}$) floods in, and CaMKII is activated. That calcium signal is the fork in the road:

- **Reinforce:** pull hidden AMPA receptors to the surface (more AMPA = stronger, faster response next time).
- **Expand:** drive gene expression for new dendritic spines (new structure).

The two notes in this folder are the operational protocols for each branch.
