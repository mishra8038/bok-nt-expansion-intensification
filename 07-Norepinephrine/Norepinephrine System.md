# Norepinephrine System

Norepinephrine (noradrenaline) is the brain's primary arousal and stress transmitter. It is synthesized in the **locus coeruleus** (the brain's main noradrenergic nucleus) and projects widely to the cortex, hippocampus, amygdala, and basal forebrain. It is the central mediator of the "fight-or-flight" response and the key driver of sustained attention, vigilance, and signal-to-noise ratio in cortical circuits.

## Synthesis and Degradation

- **Precursor:** L-Phenylalanine → L-Tyrosine → L-DOPA → Dopamine → **Norepinephrine** (via dopamine β-hydroxylase) → Epinephrine (in the adrenal medulla).
- **Rate-limiting step:** Tyrosine hydroxylase (TH), the same enzyme that limits dopamine synthesis.
- **Degradation:** MAO-A and MAO-B (intracellular), COMT (extracellular).
- **Reuptake:** NET (norepinephrine transporter) clears NE from the cleft.

## Roles

- **Arousal and attention.** NE sharpens attention and enhances the signal-to-noise ratio in prefrontal cortex. The relationship is **inverted-U**: too little NE = inattention; too much = anxiety and cognitive rigidity.
- **Modulation of glutamate.** NE modulates glutamatergic transmission in the prefrontal cortex; it can enhance or suppress LTP depending on the circuit and the level of arousal.
- **Gating of dopamine.** NE gates dopamine release in the striatum and prefrontal cortex; the two systems are tightly coupled.
- **Stress response.** NE is the primary neurotransmitter of the sympathetic nervous system; chronic elevation leads to stress, anxiety, and burnout.
- **Memory consolidation.** NE in the locus coeruleus–prefrontal circuit is critical for encoding and consolidating emotionally salient memories.

## Receptors

| Receptor | Type | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| α1 (alpha-1) | Gq-coupled | Vasoconstriction, excitatory in cortex |
| α2 (alpha-2) | Gi-coupled | Presynaptic autoreceptor; negative feedback |
| β1 (beta-1) | Gs-coupled | Heart rate, excitatory in hippocampus |
| β2 (beta-2) | Gs-coupled | Excitatory in prefrontal cortex; attention |

## Relationship to Other Systems

- **Dopamine.** NE and dopamine share a precursor (L-Tyrosine) and are often co-released. The [[Dopamine Intensification]] protocol can indirectly affect NE. Dopamine β-hydroxylase converts dopamine to NE, so the two systems are in a direct metabolic relationship.
- **Glutamate.** NE modulates glutamatergic transmission in the prefrontal cortex; it can enhance LTP at moderate levels but suppress it at high levels (inverted-U).
- **Serotonin.** NE and serotonin are both monoamines and share some regulatory mechanisms. The [[Serotonin Intensification]] protocol can indirectly affect NE.
- **GABA.** NE excites GABAergic interneurons in the prefrontal cortex, indirectly enhancing inhibitory tone.

## Safety

- L-Phenylalanine and L-Tyrosine are generally safe at 500–1000 mg.
- Excess NE can cause anxiety, insomnia, elevated blood pressure, and tachycardia.
- **Avoid combining** high-dose L-Phenylalanine or L-Tyrosine with MAO inhibitors (e.g. Selegiline) without careful monitoring, as this can precipitate a hypertensive crisis.
- **Avoid combining** with strong stimulants (amphetamines, modafinil) — see [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] for the stimulant burnout axiom.
- The inverted-U relationship means that **more is not better**: the goal is to optimize, not maximize, NE signaling.
