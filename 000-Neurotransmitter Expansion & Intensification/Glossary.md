# Glossary

Each meaning says what the term is, enough to understand it. A bare synonym is not an entry.

| Term | Meaning | See |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LTP (Long-Term Potentiation) | The lasting increase in the strength of a synapse after repeated co-activation; the cellular basis of memory reinforcement. | [[Glutamate System]] |
| NMDA receptor | An excitatory glutamate receptor gated by a magnesium ion; it opens only after the postsynaptic cell is depolarized, admitting calcium that drives plasticity. | [[Glutamate System]] |
| AMPA receptor | A fast excitatory glutamate receptor that opens immediately on binding, driving the sodium influx that depolarizes the cell. | [[Glutamate System]] |
| PAM (Positive Allosteric Modulator) | A molecule that binds a non-orthosteric site and amplifies the receptor's response to the natural transmitter without firing it artificially. | [[Positive Allosteric Modulators]] |
| Precursor | A raw material (e.g. L-Tyrosine, 5-HTP, Alpha-GPC) that raises the intracellular pool of a neurotransmitter before it is released. | [[Network Modification Paradigms]] |
| Upregulation | The homeostatic increase in receptor number or density after a period of reduced signaling, the basis of network expansion. | [[Network Modification Paradigms]] |
| Down-regulation | The homeostatic decrease in receptor number or sensitivity after prolonged over-stimulation; the failure mode the protocols avoid. | [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] |
| Excitotoxicity | Neuronal damage caused by excessive, unregulated glutamate and monoamine signaling that overwhelms cellular defenses. | [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] |
| Neurotrophic factor | A growth signal (e.g. BDNF, NGF) that drives synaptogenesis and neurogenesis, the "fertilizer" phase of expansion. | [[Glutamate System]] |
| Reuptake inhibitor | A molecule that delays the clearance of a transmitter from the synaptic cleft, lengthening its signal. | [[Serotonin System]] |
| AChE (Acetylcholinesterase) | The enzyme that breaks down acetylcholine in the synaptic cleft; inhibiting it (e.g. Huperzine A) lengthens the ACh signal. | [[Acetylcholine Intensification]] |
| MAO-B (Monoamine Oxidase B) | The enzyme that breaks down dopamine and serotonin; inhibiting it (e.g. Selegiline) lengthens the monoamine signal. | [[Dopamine Intensification]] |
| SERT (Serotonin Transporter) | The reuptake pump that clears serotonin from the cleft; blocking it (e.g. Kanna) lengthens the 5-HT signal. | [[Serotonin Intensification]] |
| GABA-T (Glutamic Acid Decarboxylase) | The enzyme that synthesizes GABA from glutamate; inhibiting it (e.g. Lemon Balm) reduces GABA breakdown. | [[GABA Intensification]] |
| Synaptogenesis | The formation of new synapses and dendritic spines; the structural outcome of network expansion. | [[Glutamate Expansion]] |
| Neurogenesis | The birth of new neurons, primarily in the hippocampus; driven by neurotrophic factors during expansion. | [[Serotonin Expansion]] |
| BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor) | A growth factor that drives synaptogenesis and neurogenesis; the "fertilizer" of the expansion phase. | [[Glutamate Expansion]] |
| OTC (Over-The-Counter) | A substance available without a prescription; this book substitutes OTC / herbal / supplement / amino-acid / vitamin agents for the framework's prescription-only compounds. | [[Positive Allosteric Modulators]] |
| Half-Life Rule | The rule that precursor loading must peak 60–90 minutes before a PAM or signal enhancer is introduced, or the enzyme sits empty and the system crashes. | [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] |
| Upregulation Lag | The 4-to-6-week period required for true receptor upregulation and structural growth; short agonist bursts only produce temporary hyper-focus followed by down-regulation. | [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] |
| Stimulant Burnout Axiom | The rule that combining heavy NMDA/AMPA or monoamine enhancers with strong stimulants bypasses safety limits, precipitating excitotoxicity and brain fog. | [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] |
| Cholinergic crisis | The adverse state produced by excessive acetylcholine (nausea, bradycardia, muscle tension); avoided by careful dosing and off-cycles. | [[Acetylcholine Intensification]] |
| Serotonin syndrome | The adverse state produced by excessive serotonin (agitation, hyperthermia, tachycardia); avoided by careful dosing and off-cycles. | [[Serotonin Intensification]] |
| Glycine | The smallest amino acid; obligate co-agonist of the NMDA receptor and a major inhibitory transmitter. Without it, the NMDA channel is silent and LTP cannot occur. | [[Glycine System]] |
| $GlyR$ (Glycine receptor) | A chloride channel that provides fast inhibition, functionally analogous to $GABA_A$; especially prominent in the spinal cord and brainstem. | [[Glycine System]] |
| Taurine | An amino sulfonic acid that can substitute for glycine at the NMDA co-agonist site, extending the window of NMDA availability. | [[Glycine Intensification]] |
| Histamine | The brain's primary wakefulness and attention transmitter; it gates the release of acetylcholine and dopamine. Antihistamines blunt ACh and dopamine protocols. | [[Modulatory Systems]] |
| Norepinephrine | The brain's primary arousal and stress transmitter; it modulates glutamate and dopamine. Chronic elevation leads to stress and burnout. | [[Modulatory Systems]] |
| L-Histidine | Amino acid precursor for histamine synthesis; OTC supplement. | [[Modulatory Systems]] |
| L-Phenylalanine | Amino acid precursor for norepinephrine (and dopamine) synthesis; OTC supplement. | [[Modulatory Systems]] |
| Norepinephrine System | The brain's primary arousal and stress transmitter; modulates glutamate and dopamine. | [[Norepinephrine System]] |
| β2 receptor | Gs-coupled norepinephrine receptor in the prefrontal cortex; central to attention and the inverted-U relationship. | [[Norepinephrine System]] |
| Inverted-U | The relationship between NE level and cognitive performance: too little = inattention; too much = anxiety and rigidity. | [[Norepinephrine Intensification]] |
| Endocannabinoid (eCB) | A lipid-based retrograde messenger (2-AG, anandamide) that modulates the release of all other NT systems. | [[Endocannabinoid System]] |
| CB1 receptor | G-protein coupled receptor on presynaptic terminals; the primary eCB receptor; inhibits neurotransmitter release. | [[Endocannabinoid System]] |
| FAAH (Fatty Acid Amide Hydrolase) | The enzyme that breaks down anandamide; inhibiting it (e.g. CBD, CBG) increases eCB tone. | [[Endocannabinoid Intensification]] |
| 2-AG (2-arachidonoylglycerol) | The most abundant endocannabinoid; modulates glutamate and GABA release; central to LTP and LTD. | [[Endocannabinoid System]] |
| Anandamide | The "bliss molecule"; an endocannabinoid that modulates mood, pain, and appetite. | [[Endocannabinoid System]] |
| Endogenous opioid | An endogenous peptide (β-endorphin, enkephalin, dynorphin) that binds to μ, δ, or κ opioid receptors. | [[Endogenous Opioid System]] |
| μ (mu) receptor | Gi/o-coupled opioid receptor; central to pain inhibition, euphoria, and reward. | [[Endogenous Opioid System]] |
| δ (delta) receptor | Gi/o-coupled opioid receptor; central to pain inhibition, mood, and reward. | [[Endogenous Opioid System]] |
| κ (kappa) receptor | Gi/o-coupled opioid receptor; central to pain modulation and dysphoria (at high levels). | [[Endogenous Opioid System]] |
| β-Endorphin | An endogenous opioid derived from POMC; central to pain inhibition, euphoria, and stress response. | [[Endogenous Opioid System]] |
| N-Acetylcysteine (NAC) | Amino acid derivative; glutathione precursor; modulates glutamate and opioid tone. | [[Endogenous Opioid Intensification]] |
| Ashwagandha | Herbal adaptogen; modulates HPA axis and opioid tone. | [[Endogenous Opioid Intensification]] |
