# Endogenous Opioid Intensification

Endogenous opioid intensification reinforces existing opioid signaling: it enhances pain inhibition, improves mood and reward, and strengthens the stress-response modulation that comes from the endogenous opioid system. The goal is to optimize opioid tone without over-sedation or over-suppression of other systems.

## Protocol

The sequence follows the universal kinetic rules in [[Universal Kinetic Rules]]: load the precursor first, then introduce the signal enhancer 60–90 minutes later.

1. **Load the precursor.** L-Tyrosine 500–1000 mg (fasted, morning) + N-Acetylcysteine (NAC) 600 mg. L-Tyrosine supports dopamine and norepinephrine synthesis (which indirectly supports opioid tone), and NAC modulates glutamate and supports glutathione synthesis.
2. **Introduce the signal enhancer (60–90 min later).** Curcumin 500–1000 mg (with piperine 5–10 mg for bioavailability) + Ashwagandha 300–600 mg (standardized to 5% withanolides). These adaptogens modulate the HPA axis and support endogenous opioid tone.
3. **Behavioral trigger.** The most potent natural trigger for endorphin release is **aerobic exercise** (20–30 min of moderate-intensity cardio) or **meditation / deep breathing** (10–20 min). This should be done 60–90 minutes after the precursor load, to coincide with the peak of the signal enhancer.

## Cycle

- **Duration:** 4–6 weeks.
- **Off-cycle:** 1–2 weeks with no tyrosine, NAC, curcumin, or ashwagandha.
- **Do not combine** with prescription opioids or MAO inhibitors in the same window — see [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] for the stimulant burnout axiom.
- **Monitor for over-sedation:** drowsiness, GI discomfort, or reduced motivation. If these occur, reduce the dose or take an off-cycle sooner.

## Safety

- L-Tyrosine is generally safe at 500–1000 mg.
- NAC is generally safe at 600–1200 mg.
- Curcumin is generally safe at 500–1000 mg.
- Ashwagandha is generally safe at 300–600 mg, but can cause mild GI discomfort or insomnia in sensitive individuals.
- **Avoid combining** with prescription opioids without medical supervision.
- The endogenous opioid system is a **tone-setting** system: the goal is to optimize, not maximize, opioid signaling.
