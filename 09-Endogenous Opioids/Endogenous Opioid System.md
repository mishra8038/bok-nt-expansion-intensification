# Endogenous Opioid System

The endogenous opioid system is the brain's primary **pain modulation and reward** system. It consists of three major endogenous peptides — **endorphins** (β-endorphin), **enkephalins** (met-enkephalin, leu-enkephalin), and **dynorphins** — which bind to three major opioid receptors: **μ (mu)**, **δ (delta)**, and **κ (kappa)**. The system is central to pain inhibition, reward, mood, stress response, and the "runner's high" / "meditation high" that comes from sustained physical or mental effort.

## Endogenous Opioids

| Opioid | Primary Receptor | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| β-Endorphin | μ (mu) | Pain inhibition, euphoria, stress response |
| Met-Enkephalin | μ (mu), δ (delta) | Pain inhibition, reward |
| Leu-Enkephalin | μ (mu), δ (delta) | Pain inhibition, reward |
| Dynorphin | κ (kappa) | Pain modulation, dysphoria (at high levels) |

## Receptors

| Receptor | Type | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| μ (mu) | Gi/o-coupled | Pain inhibition, euphoria, reward; the primary target of morphine and heroin |
| δ (delta) | Gi/o-coupled | Pain inhibition, mood, reward; the primary target of pentazocine |
| κ (kappa) | Gi/o-coupled | Pain modulation, dysphoria (at high levels); the primary target of nalbuphine |

## Synthesis and Degradation

- **Precursor:** β-Endorphin is derived from **proopiomelanocortin (POMC)**, a large precursor protein that is cleaved by prohormone convertases.
- **Enkephalins** are derived from **preproenkephalin** and **preprodynorphin**.
- **Degradation:** Endogenous opioids are broken down by **neprilysin** (for enkephalins) and **cathepsins** (for β-endorphin).

## Roles

- **Pain inhibition.** The endogenous opioid system is the brain's primary endogenous analgesic; it suppresses pain signaling in the spinal cord, brainstem, and cortex.
- **Reward and euphoria.** μ-opioid receptors in the nucleus accumbens and ventral tegmental area are central to the reward circuit; they mediate the "high" from exercise, meditation, music, and social bonding.
- **Stress response.** β-Endorphin is released during stress and physical exertion; it modulates the HPA axis and the sympathetic nervous system.
- **Mood regulation.** The endogenous opioid system is tightly coupled to the dopamine and serotonin systems; low opioid tone is associated with depression and chronic pain.
- **Social bonding.** Oxytocin and endogenous opioids work together to mediate social bonding, trust, and attachment.

## Relationship to Other Systems

- **Dopamine.** The endogenous opioid system and the dopamine system are tightly coupled in the reward circuit. μ-Opioid receptors in the VTA inhibit GABAergic interneurons, disinhibiting dopamine neurons and enhancing dopamine release. The [[Dopamine Intensification]] protocol is modulated by opioid tone.
- **Serotonin.** The endogenous opioid system and the serotonin system are coupled in mood regulation. Low opioid tone is associated with depression and chronic pain. The [[Serotonin Intensification]] protocol is modulated by opioid tone.
- **Glutamate.** Endogenous opioids suppress presynaptic glutamate release in the spinal cord and cortex; they are central to pain inhibition. The [[Glutamate Intensification]] protocol is modulated by opioid tone.
- **GABA.** Endogenous opioids modulate GABAergic transmission in the VTA and nucleus accumbens; they are central to the balance of excitation and inhibition in the reward circuit. The [[GABA Intensification]] protocol is modulated by opioid tone.
- **Endocannabinoids.** The endogenous opioid system and the eCB system are tightly coupled in pain modulation and reward. The [[Endocannabinoid Intensification]] protocol is modulated by opioid tone.
- **Norepinephrine.** Endogenous opioids modulate NE release in the locus coeruleus; they are central to stress and arousal regulation. The [[Norepinephrine Intensification]] protocol is modulated by opioid tone.

## Safety

- L-Tyrosine and L-Phenylalanine are generally safe at 500–1000 mg.
- NAC is generally safe at 600–1200 mg.
- Curcumin is generally safe at 500–1000 mg.
- Capsaicin is generally safe at 100–500 mg, but can cause GI discomfort in sensitive individuals.
- Ashwagandha and Rhodiola are generally safe at the doses listed, but can cause mild GI discomfort or insomnia in sensitive individuals.
- **Avoid combining** with prescription opioids (e.g. morphine, oxycodone) without medical supervision.
- The endogenous opioid system is a **tone-setting** system: the goal is to optimize, not maximize, opioid signaling.
