# Endogenous Opioid Expansion

Endogenous opioid expansion builds new opioid structure: it increases μ and δ receptor density (more robust pain inhibition and reward capacity) and strengthens the endogenous opioid pathways that modulate stress and mood. The goal is to create a brain that has a highly responsive endogenous opioid system without over-sedation or over-suppression of other systems.

## Protocol

The sequence follows the universal kinetic rules in [[Universal Kinetic Rules]]: intermittent antagonism to trigger upregulation, then growth stimulation, then dense influx.

1. **Intermittent antagonism (days 1–5).** Reduce opioid precursor loading for 2–3 days (e.g. skip L-Tyrosine and NAC) to trigger homeostatic upregulation of μ and δ receptors. This is the "starve to grow" phase.
2. **Growth stimulation (daily).** Lion's Mane 1000 mg + Uridine Monophosphate (UMP) 250–500 mg + DHA 500–1000 mg. These neurotrophic factors drive synaptogenesis at opioid synapses and strengthen the endogenous opioid pathways.
3. **Dense influx (days 6+).** Resume L-Tyrosine 500–1000 mg + NAC 600 mg + Curcumin 500–1000 mg + Ashwagandha 300–600 mg. This floods the newly upregulated receptors with endogenous opioids, locking in the structural gains.

## Cycle

- **Duration:** 4–6 weeks.
- **Off-cycle:** 1–2 weeks with no tyrosine, NAC, curcumin, or ashwagandha.
- **Do not combine** with prescription opioids or MAO inhibitors in the same window — see [[Universal Kinetic Rules]] for the stimulant burnout axiom.
- **Monitor for over-sedation:** drowsiness, GI discomfort, or reduced motivation. If these occur, reduce the dose or take an off-cycle sooner.

## Safety

- L-Tyrosine is generally safe at 500–1000 mg.
- NAC is generally safe at 600–1200 mg.
- The intermittent antagonism phase (days 1–5) should not exceed 3 days without opioid precursors, to avoid excessive down-regulation and impaired pain inhibition.
- **Avoid combining** with prescription opioids without medical supervision.
- The endogenous opioid system is a **tone-setting** system: the goal is to optimize, not maximize, opioid signaling.
